# Stage 2: Charter

The charter is the agent's identity, written once with the owner and changed only by the owner. It is not the state page: the state page changes every session, the charter almost never.

## To the AI reading this

Start from `templates/charter.md`. Use the discovery decision from stage 1 as input; do not ask again what is already answered there. Write the charter where context-system keeps standing pages (in the GitHub layout, `context/charter.md`, with the `description` and `updated` header context-system requires). In another store, put it where the person keeps their standing pages, and tell them where.

Work **part by part**. For each part: draft it in the owner's words, show it, ask "is this right?", change it until they say yes, then move on. One part at a time; one question at a time.

1. **Identity.** What the agent is, in terms of the concern. One or two sentences, plus a line saying the tools are what the agent is practised with today, and that swapping one would not make it a different agent.
2. **Mandate.** One line, written as the *first line* of the section: why it exists and what the owner can then trust or do. Later stages copy this line word for word.
3. **Owned spine.** The one to three things it must always keep alive. For each, ask: "What does it look like when this is broken?" and write the answer.
4. **Scope boundaries.** *In scope*: what it does. *Not mine*: kinds of work that are not its. Write each "not mine" line as a plain statement ending "Not mine." Do not add a place to send the work. If the owner asks where such work goes, say: "You decide, each time. This agent just says it is not its work." Do not build anything to carry it.
5. **Sharp test.** The question from stage 1, as the *first line* of the section. Later stages copy it word for word.

Then two additions:

6. **The change rule.** Use the template's wording: a session may propose a change, only the owner makes one, and the change is recorded with the date and the owner's words. Read it to them and ask for a yes. Do not soften it.
7. **Current season.** Ask: "What is true about how you work *right now* that might not be true in three months?" Put those things in the dated "Current season" section, with today's date and a review date the owner picks. This section is kept apart on purpose. Without it, a passing way of working gets written into the identity and the charter goes stale.

Leave the Toolset section as the template has it: stage 3 fills it in.

## Ratify

Read the whole charter back. Ask: "Do you ratify this as the charter, as it stands?" Only an explicit yes counts; "looks fine" is not a yes, so ask again in those words. Then write the ratification line at the top ("Ratified by the owner on <date>, in so many words") and the first entry in "Record of changes". If you cannot get a yes today, leave the charter marked "Draft, not ratified" and stop the install here.

Rebuild the index if context-system gave you a script for it.

## Check

The person can open the charter and see: five headed parts, the change rule, a "Current season" section with a date, and a ratification line with a date. You read it back to them and they said yes.
